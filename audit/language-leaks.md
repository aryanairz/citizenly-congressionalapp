# Language leak audit - bundled question bank

Generated by `scripts/audit-language-leaks.js`. **Report only - nothing was translated or rewritten.**

Source: `src/data/questions.json` - 128 questions × 48 languages × 6 fields (prompt, 4 options, explanation) = **36,864** strings, of which **36,096** are translations.

## What each category means

| Category | Meaning | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| `IDENTICAL` | Byte-identical to English, and unchanged in **4 or fewer** of the 47 languages | **High** - near-certain leak |
| `IDENTICAL_MAYBE` | Byte-identical, unchanged in 5–14 languages | Medium - leak, or a term that genuinely doesn't translate |
| `ASCII_IN_NON_LATIN` | Over half the letters are ASCII in a non-Latin-script language | **High** |
| `ENGLISH_TOKENS` | English function words as standalone tokens inside a non-Latin string | **High** |
| `LENGTH` | Under 40% or over 250% of the English length | Low - rough truncation detector, many false positives |
| `MISSING` | Field absent or empty | **Critical** |

### How proper nouns are separated from leaks

Rather than a hand-curated name list (which silently misses whatever nobody thought of), this counts **how many of the 47 languages leave a value byte-identical**. "Joe Biden" is unchanged in ~30 languages because it is a name; "Capitalism" is unchanged in exactly one, because one translator skipped it. A value unchanged in more than 14 languages is treated as a proper noun and listed in the appendix instead of counted as a leak.

`LENGTH` is not reported for Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Thai or Khmer, where a much shorter string is normal.
